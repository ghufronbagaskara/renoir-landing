## 2. Where this comes from

Renoir did not start as a business idea. It started as a pattern its founder kept seeing from the inside.

While still completing a computer science degree, he was already building production systems for real companies — internal tracking tools, operational dashboards, monitoring platforms, order systems. Not mockups, not coursework. Software that people at those companies opened every morning and depended on to do their jobs.

Doing that work repeatedly surfaced three problems, and Renoir exists to solve exactly those three.

**One: most businesses buy a website, not an outcome.**
They pay for a theme dressed in their logo. It looks like four hundred other sites, it loads slowly on the phones their customers actually use, search engines struggle to make sense of it, and nobody inside the company can change a word without calling someone. It exists. It does not work.

**Two: the handover is where projects die.**
A vendor builds something, sends a zip file or a repository, and leaves. The client is now holding software they cannot deploy, on infrastructure nobody configured, with no monitoring and no one to call when it stops. The build was the easy part. Running it is the part that was never included.

**Three: splitting the work across vendors costs more than it saves.**
A design agency, a development shop, a hosting provider. Three contracts, three timelines, three interpretations of the same brief — and when something breaks, three parties explaining why it is one of the others' fault. Nobody owns the result, because the result was never one person's job.

Renoir is built as the answer to all three: **one team that designs it, writes it from scratch, puts it into production, and keeps it running.**

## 3. What Renoir believes

These are the positions behind every decision. Use them to keep copy consistent; do not quote them verbatim on the site.

- **Software that works and software worth looking at are the same requirement, not two.** Most studios are good at one. The split is a business's problem, not something it should have to solve.
- **Performance and discoverability are specifications, not upgrades.** A site that loads in four seconds is not finished. It is broken in a way that has been normalised.
- **Nothing is delivered until it is running.** A repository is not a deliverable. A URL that stays up is.
- **Written from scratch beats assembled from parts.** Page builders and themes are faster to start and slower forever after.
- **The person who builds it should be the person you talk to.** Layers of account management exist to protect agencies, not clients.

## 4. What Renoir does

Three services. Everything offered falls into one of these; anything that does not is declined or referred.

### 4.1 Marketing & Landing Sites

Websites written from scratch for companies that need to be found and taken seriously. Built on modern frameworks with static rendering where possible, optimised images, and minimal client-side weight. Technical SEO is part of the build, not a later project: semantic structure, structured data, crawlable routing, clean metadata, sitemaps, and correct indexing from day one — including how the site is read by AI search and answer engines.

_For companies whose website is the first thing a prospect judges them by._

### 4.2 Custom Internal Systems

Business software built around how a company actually works, rather than forcing the company around software it rented. Order and document tracking, operational dashboards, activity monitoring, approval workflows, internal tooling, integrations between systems that were never designed to talk to each other.

_For companies running operations on spreadsheets, WhatsApp threads, and paper — or on a tool that fits someone else's business._

### 4.3 Deployment & Care

Infrastructure, deployment pipelines, monitoring, backups, and ongoing maintenance. Included in every build and available as a standing retainer afterwards. This is not an add-on service; it is the reason anything above stays useful after launch.

_For every client, by default._

- Established small and mid-sized businesses whose operations have outgrown off-the-shelf tools
- Companies whose current website actively undersells them
- Businesses that need one accountable party, not a vendor committee
- Clients who want to talk to the person building the thing

**Not a fit — and worth saying so early:**

- Template or page-builder work, or maintaining someone else's template
- Projects with no defined decision-maker
- Work where design and engineering are expected to be handled by separate vendors
- Engagements without a maintenance path after launch

Turning down poor-fit work is a feature. Renoir takes few projects and finishes them.

## 6. What makes Renoir different

Five points. These are the proof arguments used in proposals, pitches, and the website.

1. **Written from zero.** No themes, no page builders, no plugin stacks. Every line exists because the project needed it — which is why the results are fast and why they stay maintainable.
2. **Performance and discoverability are part of the spec.** Targets are set before the build starts and measured before handover. If the numbers are not met, it is not finished.
3. **Design and engineering under one roof.** The designer and the engineer are on the same team with the same deadline, so nothing is lost translating an idea into a build.
4. **Delivered running, not delivered as files.** Server, deployment pipeline, domain, SSL, monitoring, backups — configured and handed over working, with an optional retainer so it stays that way.
5. **Direct access.** The person who writes the code is the person in the meeting. Small team, short lines, no account manager relaying decisions.

## 8. How Renoir works — the process

Four stages. Used on the website, in proposals, and in client conversations.

**01 · Scope**
A conversation about the business problem before anything about technology. What breaks today, what it costs, who decides, and what a finished result would look like. Ends with a written scope and a fixed price for that scope.

**02 · Design**
Structure first, surface second. Information architecture, flows, then interface. The client sees and approves the design before a line of production code is written.

**03 · Build**
Written from scratch against the approved design. Progress is visible on a live staging URL throughout — no month-long silences, no reveal at the end.

**04 · Deploy & Care**
Infrastructure configured, pipeline set up, monitoring and backups in place, domain and SSL live. Handover includes documentation and a walkthrough. Care continues on a retainer, or the client takes it from there — their choice, made with full access either way.

## 9. Voice & tone

Renoir writes the way a competent person talks when they are not trying to impress anyone.

**Do:**

- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Concrete nouns and active verbs. "We configure the server" beats "server configuration is handled."
- Second person. Address the reader as _you_.
- State outcomes, not adjectives about ourselves. Let the reader conclude we are good.
- Use specific numbers where real numbers exist. Say nothing where they do not.
- Admit limits plainly. "We don't do that" is a stronger sentence than a hedge.
- Be matter-of-fact about being small. Small is the advantage; never apologise for it and never explain it away.

**Don't:**

- No exclamation marks. No emoji in client-facing copy.
- No superlatives about ourselves — _leading, premier, world-class, best-in-class_.
- No hype verbs — _revolutionise, transform, disrupt, unlock, empower, elevate, supercharge_.
- No throat-clearing openers — _"In today's digital landscape…"_, _"In an increasingly connected world…"_
- No stacked adjectives. If two words do the job of five, use two.
- Never mention team members' ages, student status, or academic institutions anywhere client-facing.
- Never mention the tools, software, or methods used to produce work.

**Register by context:**

- _Website_ — confident, spare, a little cool. Short paragraphs, generous spacing.
- _Proposals_ — precise and structured. Numbered, scannable, no persuasion language; the structure does the persuading.
- _Email & chat_ — direct and warm. Answer first, context second.
- _Social_ — show the work, say little. One sentence and an image beats a caption.

### FAQ

**Why aren't prices listed?**
Because the same page can be a two-week job or a two-month one depending on what sits behind it. We scope first, then quote a fixed price for that scope. You will never get an invoice larger than the number you agreed to.

Yes. Full ownership of all source code and design assets transfers to you on final payment. No licensing, no lock-in, no hostage hosting.

**What if I want to leave?**
You take everything — repository, infrastructure access, documentation — and go. We would rather you stay because it works than because leaving is difficult.

**How long does a project take?**
A marketing site is typically two to four weeks. Internal systems depend entirely on scope; we give a timeline with the written scope, before you commit.

**Do you maintain what you build?**
Yes, on a monthly retainer covering hosting, monitoring, backups, updates, and a defined amount of change work. Optional, but most clients keep it.

**Do you work with clients outside Indonesia?**
Yes. We work remotely by default and are used to working across time zones.

## 14. Visual direction

Brief for producing the logo, brand kit, and any generated imagery.

**Principle:** a gallery, not a tech company. Restraint, space, and one confident accent. The work on the page should be the loudest thing; the brand should be the quietest.

**Logotype:** a wordmark — the word _Renoir_ set in type, not a symbol. Reasons: the name is the asset and needs teaching, not abstracting; a wordmark works at every size without a separate icon; and it avoids the generic geometric mark every studio has. A monogram **R** may be derived from the same typeface for favicon and avatar use.

**Type direction:**

- Display / wordmark: a high-contrast serif or a refined grotesque with editorial character. Reference feel: gallery signage and exhibition catalogues, not SaaS.
- Body: a clean, highly legible sans at generous line height.
- Never more than two typefaces.

**Palette A — Bone & Ink** _(recommended)_

- Bone `#F4F1EA` — primary background
- Ink `#14161A` — primary text
- Cobalt `#1B3FCC` — single accent, used sparingly
- Stone `#8A8880` — secondary text, rules

**Rules:**

- One accent colour only. Never two.
- No gradients, no glassmorphism, no glow, no drop shadows on text.
- Photography and screenshots of real work over illustration. If illustration is needed, line work only.
- Whitespace is the primary design element. When in doubt, add margin.
- Never use impressionist paintings, brushstroke textures, or paint-splatter motifs. The name is a reference to craft, not a costume.

**Prompt seed for generated brand imagery:**

> Minimal editorial brand system for a design and engineering studio. Off-white bone background, near-black text, one deep cobalt accent. Gallery signage aesthetic: generous whitespace, high-contrast serif wordmark, thin rules. No gradients, no glow, no 3D, no paint or brushstroke motifs, no stock tech imagery.
