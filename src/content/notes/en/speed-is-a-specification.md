Performance is often treated as polish: build the site first, make it fast later. Later rarely arrives. By the time anyone measures, the site is live, the budget is spent, and speeding it up means undoing decisions made months earlier.

A slow site isn't waiting for an upgrade. It's a broken site that people have learned to tolerate.

## Why speed decides first impressions

Visitors don't compare your load time with a benchmark. They compare it with their patience. A page that stays blank, jumps while loading, or ignores the first tap feels careless — and that feeling transfers to the business behind it. For a firm that sells expertise, the website is often the first proof of how it works.

## What to measure

You don't need to be an engineer to ask for the right targets. Three questions cover most of it:

- **How quickly does the main content appear?** The largest thing a visitor sees should show up fast on a mid-range phone, not just on a developer's laptop.
- **Does the layout stay still?** Text and buttons shouldn't jump around as images and fonts load.
- **Does it respond straight away?** Taps and clicks should react immediately, even while the page is still loading.

## Being found is part of the spec

Speed matters little if nobody finds the page. Search engines, and increasingly AI answer engines, need to understand what a site is about: one clear main heading per page, descriptive titles and summaries, structured data that names the business and its services, a sitemap, and pages that can be read without running heavy scripts. None of this can be bolted on cleanly afterwards.

## Writing targets into the scope

The fix is simple: put the targets in the scope before the build starts.

1. Agree on performance targets for the key pages, measured on mobile.
2. Agree on indexing requirements: structure, metadata, structured data, sitemap.
3. Check both before handover, and share the results.
4. If the targets aren't met, the project isn't finished.

When speed and discoverability are written down as requirements, they get designed in from the first decision instead of argued about at the end.
