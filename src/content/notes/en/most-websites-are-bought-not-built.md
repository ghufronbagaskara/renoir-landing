A theme is a finished website that someone else designed for nobody in particular. You add a logo, swap the photos, rewrite the headings, and launch. It looks fine. It also looks like every other business that bought the same theme — and prospects notice, even when they can't say why.

The cost isn't the purchase price. It's the first impression you don't get to make.

## Slow on the phones that matter

Themes are built to handle every possible use: sliders you'll never enable, fonts you'll never pick, layouts for businesses you aren't. All of it ships to your visitors anyway. On a fast office connection it barely shows. On a mid-range phone on mobile data — where many of your customers actually are — the page hangs long enough for them to leave.

A site written for your content only carries what your content needs.

## Hard for search engines to read

Search engines and AI answer engines read structure: headings in order, clear page titles and descriptions, and structured data that says what your business is and where it works. Themes tend to get this approximately right for everyone and exactly right for no one. Headings are chosen for how they look. Important text sits inside images or scripts. The result is a site that exists but doesn't get found.

## Nobody can change a word

Many theme-based sites end up with one person who knows how to edit them — often someone outside the company. A price changes, a service is added, a phone number moves, and the update waits in someone's inbox. A website that can't keep up with the business slowly stops describing it.

## When a theme is the right call

Sometimes it is. If you need a page up this week for an event, or you're testing an idea before you commit to it, a theme is a reasonable shortcut. The problem starts when the shortcut becomes the permanent home of a business that has outgrown it.

## What changes when a site is written from scratch

When a site is written for one business:

- it carries only the code that business needs, so it loads quickly on real devices;
- its structure is designed for how people and search engines read it;
- performance and indexing targets are agreed before the build and checked before launch;
- the people who need to update it can, because editing was part of the scope.

## Questions to ask before you buy

- Will this be built from a theme or page builder, or written for us?
- What speed and search targets will you commit to, and how will you show we met them?
- Who can change the content after launch, and how?
- Who owns the code and the hosting when the project ends?

If the answers are vague, the website probably will be too.
